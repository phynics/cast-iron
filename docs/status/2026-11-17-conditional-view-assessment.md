# Conditional-view assessment: scores from the saved frames, 2026-11-17

Refs [#448](https://github.com/phynics/cast-iron/issues/448), part of [#443](https://github.com/phynics/cast-iron/issues/443). This is a reading of the saved pixels of the 40 conditional-view frames (every scenario, phone 390×844 and desktop 1280×900, light and dark), made with the frames' [manifest](../research/capture-evidence/448-conditional-views.json) open. It is screenshot evidence from one Linux host: no CI, no deployment, no device. The `ios-guide` frames are **WebKit engine simulation** with an iPhone Safari user agent, not iOS Safari; physical-device acceptance stays with [#423](https://github.com/phynics/cast-iron/issues/423). The frames are in the [evidence archive](https://github.com/phynics/cast-iron/tree/42e9a57eab1dad150fe899f356f23d24e96a8132) (commit `42e9a57`, all 104 files verified byte for byte against the originals).

## Scale

I found no written council rubric and no council scores for these views, in the repository or on #443, so this is one assessor's 1 to 5 scale and is **not comparable** with the council's scores for the 67 earlier states. 5: needs no change. 4: works, with a minor gap. 3: works, with a gap a reader notices. 2: the state is understandable but leaves the reader without a next step. 1: blocks or misleads. Colour contrast was judged by eye, not computed. A pair of scores is phone / desktop; light and dark gave the same score in every view.

Only 30 of the 40 files differ as pixels. The reading-header frames of the three update groups are byte-identical (12 files, 4 distinct), and the phone settings frames of `library-update` and `combined-updates` are byte-identical (4 files, 2 distinct). That is what the app draws, not a capture fault: the header action shows one pip or pill for any waiting update and the accessible name (not visible in a frame) says which kind (`crates/web/src/views/header_action.rs`).

## Masthead check

The 36 frames that expect a masthead all show it (manifest `masthead_in_frame: true`, 61 px, sticky, hit test lands on the header), and I confirmed this on the pixels. The four `storage-failure` frames have no header by design. In the four `guide-dialog` frames the header is present but dimmed by the modal backdrop. I also looked at three diagnostic frames: `desktop-search-scroll1500-viewport` carries the masthead; `desktop-search-scroll1500-full-page` has none at the top and the header bar sits mid-image at the scroll offset; `phone-class-scroll1500-full-page` has none at the top. This agrees with the [diagnosis](2026-11-17-conditional-view-captures.md): a capture artifact of full-page shots while scrolled, not a navigation defect.

## Product defects

None is supported by these frames. Two things that look like defects in them are not:

- **The 30 px phone update button.** The Chromium phone frames are a narrow viewport without touch emulation, so the header action shows its fine-pointer size. `web/styles.css` gives coarse pointers a 44 px button, and the WebKit touch frames show that size. No defect.
- **WebKit console messages.** The 12 WebKit frames record 1, 2 and 3 cumulative `Refused to apply a stylesheet because its hash, its nonce, or 'unsafe-inline' ...` messages; no Chromium frame records any. The manifest lists it as a known artifact and no frame shows a visual effect. I did not identify the stylesheet, so this is a question for the physical-device work in #423, not a defect.

Proposals the frames support, for the adopt, defer or drop evaluation in [#447](https://github.com/phynics/cast-iron/issues/447) and not as defects: a next step on the storage-failure screen (and the raw `SecurityError` text kept out of the sentence); one statement instead of two of "your stars are kept" and one mention of the version in the library offer; a readable label for the library version.
